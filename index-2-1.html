Every piece passes through five hands.

A dyer works the yarn in small batches, so no two skeins land the same. A weaver or knitter builds the piece on frame or loom over one to three days. A finisher steams, blocks and hand-stitches every seam and fringe. A quality-checker checks for tension before anything ships. And you decide where it lives.

A small studio, five sets of hands.

Shopwovenart started on one loom in a spare room, dyeing leftover cotton because the colors on offer never felt right. Ten years on we're still small on purpose — every piece is still touched by a person, not a machine, from skein to stitch.

Why we weave

Machine-made textiles look flawless because a person never touches them. We like the opposite — a slightly uneven row is proof someone made it.

2

Who's behind it

A dyer, two weavers, a finisher and one person answering every message you send us — that's the whole team.

Where we work

One workshop, one set of looms, small batches only — we'd rather sell out of a colorway than water it down.
